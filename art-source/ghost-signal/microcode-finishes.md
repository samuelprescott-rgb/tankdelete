# Ghost Signal: art sources for the playable material pass

These are original generated surface paintings used by meshes, not screenshots or evidence of gameplay. The day sky is registered to the world key and also sampled in water reflections. The four field finishes supply building and rainy-courtyard surfaces. The micro-ASCII is a separate, seeded, local material baked from fifteen hand-specified 5×7 glyphs, filtered with distance.

## Field finishes source: field-finishes-v2.png

Use case: stylized-concept. A single square seamless RGB albedo texture atlas for a real-time 3D pixel art video game in a Vietnam river village and rain-damaged embassy. Full bleed 2 by 2 exact equal square quadrants touching directly, no frames, labels, padding, text, people, no perspective; all 4 orthographic planar seamless tiling material swatches. Upper left: fine interlocking woven strips of honey-colored split bamboo in diagonal basketweave, several subtle alternating aged strips and softly dulled black crevices. Upper right: thatched palm roofing viewed straight down, thin, vertically oriented tied and overlapping palm fronds that taper down, rich straw, muted bronze, clay, umber; natural irregular ends, not a repeating checker pattern. Lower left: shattered ivory-gray weathered lime plaster revealing localized patches of warm small fired bricks and dark mortar; broad quiet areas of gray intact wall, fine rain streaks and small fine chips, not a perfectly uniform brick wall. Lower right: uneven rain-dark grey courtyard asphalt with irregular light aggregate, tar cracks and small pale straw / concrete flecks; nonreflective diffuse detail only, no painted highlights AND no big leaf clusters. All four are deliberate premium cinematic pixel art at fine scale like a beautifully handcrafted late-90s adventure remastered in 3D, tiny hard-edged shapes, grouped pigment clusters, limited muted palette, flat neutral even illumination, excellent individually readable materials. No cast shadows, directional lights, vignette or frames, no gradients. Avoid global monochrome and never show entire surroundings or 3D props. Must split exactly at 50% to allow cropping into independent square materials.

## Painted horizon source: daybreak-uplands.png

Use case: stylized-concept. Production skybox sky-and-distant-mountains texture only, for a 3D pixel-art game. A very wide panoramic strip of dawn in the highlands around a 1960s Vietnamese river valley, equirectangular cylindrical wrap with matching ends, 3:1 landscape. This is diffuse painted BACKGROUND art, not a scene illustration, not a screenshot, nothing in the foreground, no people, vehicles, buildings, nearby trees, paths or water. The horizon is in the lowest 20%; all of the upper ~75% is sky. Arrange a small soft golden rising sun, roughly two thirds across the width and three quarters down the image; palest salmon and pearl mist ONLY around that point, desaturated mauve-blue and dusk teal around rest of horizon. A few lifted banks of warm broken clouds lit from underneath, a wide clear lavender blue dome above, higher cloud shelf at corners; smoke not uniformly distributed. Three or four overlapping distant, richly detailed and individually shaped ridges with intricate irregular saddles, erosional gullies, and tree-grained terrain, a few very faint terraced folds but NO freestanding tabletop peaks, no rim-lit white tree cutouts, no smooth green mounds. Emphasize minute broken notches along major silhouettes and natural cool layered haze so the ridges dissolve toward horizon. Authored fine pixel clusters paired with strong value grouping, deliberate painterly pixel art, NO thick black outlines, no visible type, no prominent dither pattern, no Matrix text. Low-contrast and painterly where the near terrain of the 3D game will overlap the bottom. This texture must not include any view down from a mountain, ground in foreground, or flat fog band: only elevated distant landforms and a beautiful natural dawn sky, designed to sit behind live 3D trees.
